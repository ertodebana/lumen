Chapter 4

Operators

Arithmetic, comparison and logic, and the order in which they are applied.

Precedence

Operators lower in this table bind more tightly. All binary operators group from the left, so 10 - 4 - 3 is (10 - 4) - 3. Parentheses override everything.

LevelOperatorsMeaning
1 (loosest)=Assignment. Groups from the right.
2orLogical or.
3andLogical and.
4notLogical negation, written before its operand.
5==  !=Equal, not equal.
6<  <=  >  >=Ordering.
7+  -Add or join, subtract.
8*  /  %Multiply, divide, remainder.
9-Negation, written before its operand.
10 (tightest)f(x)Function call.

Arithmetic

-, *, / and % require two numbers. / is always floating-point division. % is the remainder, with the sign of its right-hand side. Dividing or taking a remainder by zero is an error.

Comparison

<, <=, > and >= compare two numbers, or two strings in dictionary order. Mixing the two is an error.

Logic

and and or stop as soon as the answer is known, and they give back one of their operands rather than a plain true or false.

  • a or b is a if a is truthy, otherwise b.
  • a and b is a if a is falsy, otherwise b.

The third line does not fail even though no function named missing exists: the right-hand side is never evaluated. not always gives a boolean.

When types do not fit

Lumen does not guess. Apart from + with a string and ==, an operator given the wrong kind of value stops the program and names both types.
