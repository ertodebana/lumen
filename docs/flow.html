Chapter 5

Control Flow

Two statements steer a program: if chooses, and while repeats.

if, else if, else

The condition is written without parentheses, and the braces are always required, even around a single statement.

Any value can be the condition; chapter 2 gives the truthiness rule. A short body can sit on one line:

while

while evaluates its condition, runs the block if the condition is truthy, and repeats. It is the only loop.

Counting and accumulating

The usual pattern is a counter declared before the loop and advanced at the end of the body. Here the results are gathered into one string and printed once.

Note where each variable lives. i and line are declared outside the loop, so they persist. word is declared inside the body, so each pass gets a fresh one.

Leaving a loop early

There is no break or continue. To stop in the middle of a loop, put the loop in a function and return.

To skip the rest of one pass, wrap the rest of the body in an if.

CarefulA while whose condition never becomes falsy runs forever. From the command line, Ctrl-C stops it.
